# Part 4c proposal: new industries and company names

**This is a proposal for the owner to approve. Nothing has been built.**

Written on 3 November 2026. The build stands at Part 4b (foreign markets), merged 31 October 2026.

The owner's answers, given on 3 November 2026:

- five or six new industries, twenty-four companies;
- medicines and healthcare, groceries and retail, technology and payments, film and entertainment;
- a mix of listed and private, leaning listed.

Six industries were needed to reach twenty-four companies at four each. The owner named four, so two
more are proposed below and are marked as such. If either is wrong, say so and it is dropped — nothing
is fixed until the owner says build.

## Why these six

Each one is chosen for what it does to the rest of the economy, not just for variety. A new industry is
only worth building if the existing machinery — wages, the naira, interest rates, the newsroom, the
exchange's baskets — has something new to act on.

- **Medicines and healthcare.** Nigeria imports most of its medicines. That single fact gives this
  industry a story every quarter: when the naira falls, their costs rise; when the Central Bank's
  reserves run low, they wait for dollars; when inflation runs, they cannot always pass it on. It also
  gives the newsroom a subject the game does not have yet.
- **Groceries and retail.** Closest to households of anything on the list. Their sales follow wages and
  consumer spending directly, so a squeeze on household money shows up here first.
- **Technology and payments.** Fast growing, and expensive against their profits. When the long interest
  rate rises, their shares lose the most — the opposite of banks. This is the industry that makes the
  rate cycle visible on the exchange.
- **Film and entertainment.** Ties into media, the press desk and the newsroom, which already exist.
  Gives stories a new kind of subject and gives the film and music businesses their own quarters.
- **Solid minerals and mining** (proposed). Nigeria's other mineral wealth, mostly untapped. It earns
  dollars, so it is helped by a falling naira — the opposite of medicines. Nice pairing with the oil
  industry, without being oil.
- **Fertilisers and agro-chemicals** (proposed). Sits between oil and agriculture. It takes gas as its
  raw material, so it is pulled by the gas price, and it sells to the farms that already exist. It makes
  the agriculture industry's costs move for a real reason.

## The tally

Twenty-four companies: eighteen listed, six private (one in each industry). Six industries. Three new
headquarters cities would stay as they are — all five existing cities are used, with Lagos heaviest, as
it already is.

## Questions for the owner before any building

These change the build, so they need answers first.

1. **Are the two proposed industries right?** Solid minerals and fertilisers. If you would rather have
   something else — water, education, car assembly — say so now and it is swapped.
2. **Sector baskets.** The exchange has baskets for banks, oil and gas, consumer goods and property.
   Should the six new industries get baskets of their own, so index funds and ETFs can copy them? I
   recommend yes for all six; it is what makes them useful to the funds built in Part 3.
3. **Who owns the six private ones.** Like the private companies from Part 2A, they would be owned by
   founders and bought through deals.
4. **Do the new industries get their own objectives?** Every industry so far has work attached to it for
   the three builds. I recommend each of the six gets a full set, so no build is left out.
5. **Bankruptcy and rescue.** Should these be able to fail and be rescued like the others, with their
   own news and pop-ups? I recommend yes, unchanged from the existing rules.

## Naming notes, so the owner can judge the style

- Roots are Nigerian places or words and are not reused across these six industries, though some places
  already appear elsewhere in the catalogue (Kano, Lagos, Aba) — that reuse is normal and matches the
  existing file.
- Nothing is named after a real person, a real company, or an exam body. That follows working rule 6.
- Where a root could read as a real business, it was replaced. Balogun and Sabi were both dropped for
  that reason.
