## Slice 1: the section and the trading floor screen

Everything on the screen is the player's own trading account; nobody else's is ever read.

- **The bar along the top:** realised profit and loss (on shares sold, after fees), unrealised (on shares held), the two
  together, fees paid, exposure (what the shares are worth), cash, and the account's value.
- **Market:** every share, its price and the day's move. Choosing one moves the whole screen to it.
- **News:** the newest stories with a keyword search; each is tagged with its company, and the tag chooses that share.
- **Positions and the trade blotter:** each holding with its average price, value and profit; every trade with its side,
  price and fee, newest first.
- **Exchange box:** the share, how many, and the best price waiting to sell and to buy; the deal's value and the fee are
  shown before the trade is done. A trade here is a market order on the exchange.
- **Intraday chart:** the last price in each 5, 15, 30 or 60 minutes from 12:00 to 22:00, with the day's opening price
  and the best prices waiting drawn across; before the day's first trade it shows the last day that had one, and says so.
  Pointing at it reads out the time, the price and the shares traded.

**The Auction House tab** lists every open auction (property, newspapers and radio stations, banks), then those settled in
the last two days: the leading price, what the lot was worth when it opened, how many bid, whether the seller's lowest
price has been met (never the price), the player's own bid, and the time left. Each links to where it is bid on.

`GET /api/floor/auctions`); `web/src/Floor.tsx`. Tests: `tests/test_floor.py` (4), `web/src/floor.test.ts` (3).

## Slice 2: price requests

As the owner played it in the investing challenge: a client asks banks for a price, the banks race a countdown, and the
client takes the best. The deal is done at once, away from the exchange, with no exchange fee: the bank earns the gap
between its buying and selling prices.

**The client** (any player, with their own account), on the trading floor's "Ask the banks" panel:
- chooses the share (whatever the screen is on), buy, sell, or **both ways** (a buying and a selling price, without saying
  which it wants), how many shares, and how long the banks have (15 to 60 seconds);
- asks one to five banks: the six computer banks and every player desk that is open (a desk that could not deliver
  before shows how often);
- watches the prices land live, each with how long it holds, the best marked; takes one with Buy or Sell, or passes.

**The banker** (an investment banker) opens their desk on the same screen. While it is open:
- each request pops up wherever they are in the game, with its countdown, the client's name, the exchange's best
  prices and last trade, and how many of the shares the banker holds;
- they answer with a buying price, a selling price or both (as asked) and how long it holds (10 to 60 seconds); a new
  price replaces their last;
- they deal with their own cash and shares. A banker who cannot deliver when the client takes the price is marked for
  it, publicly, and nothing moves;
- they never see another bank's price;
- computer clients (pension funds, insurers, foreign funds, a fast trader and others) call an open desk now and then,
  with two computer banks competing; at the end of their countdown they take the best price if it beats the exchange
  after its fee, or pass.

**The computer banks** each have a style: a spread from 0.4% to 1.1%, wider for a big deal, and a few seconds to answer.

**Rules:** only while the market is open; prices inside the day's limits; buying price below selling price; at most
three requests waiting for one client and 1% of a company's shares in one request; trading bans (a court's, or around
one's own story) apply as on the exchange; each deal is recorded for both sides' cost, so the profit figures stay
right. Deals show on the blotter (marked as from the Floor) and in "Your deals on the Floor".

Where: migration `055_floor_requests.sql`; `simulation/floor.py`; settings `FLOOR_*` in `simulation/world_data.py`; the

(`simulation/pings.py`, kind `floor`); `web/src/FloorDesk.tsx`. Tests: `tests/test_floor.py` (6 more).

## Slice 3: the deeper game

- **Haggling.** Beside each price the client can send a counter ("can you do 226?"), better for the client than the
  bank's price. A computer bank answers at once: yes if it still keeps at least 40% of its spread against the market
  now, no otherwise. A banker sees the counter on their desk (or in the pop-up) and accepts (the deal is done at the
  counter) or declines while their price holds.
- **Firm or indicative.** A banker may mark a price indicative: only a guide. Taking it asks the banker to confirm
  ("last look"). Declining, or not answering in time, is **backing away**, counted and shown to every client beside the
  desk's name, as are deals it could not deliver. Computer banks quote only indicatively in a fast market (a share more
  than 4% from its opening price), and confirm if the market has not moved against them.
- **The cover.** Once a deal is done, the banks that lost see the price that beat them, and the winner sees the next
  best price (what it left on the table).
- **Baskets.** Two to five shares, all bought or all sold, priced by each bank as one margin over (or under) the
  market's middle price for each share, written down when asked.
- **Blocks.** More than 1% of a company (up to 5%), worth at least ₦250 million, only as a block; once done it is
  reported to every player in "Blocks reported" (the share, the size, the price), never who.
- **Bonds.** Any bond that trades, quoted as a price with the yield it gives; a banker quotes in yields and the price is
  worked out. A bond's price on the Floor stays within 20% of its fair value; the bond desk's prices are the yardstick.
- **Chat and deal tickets.** A client may write to any bank's desk, and a banker to any client who asked their desk for
  a price; the chat is kept for good, and the Money Watch may read it. A line such as "I'll take 3,000 HMB at 46.20" is
  recognised, and either side can make it a ticket: the other has a minute to confirm, and it is done between the two
  players' own accounts.
- **Best execution.** Each of the client's deals is scored against the exchange's best price when it asked (with the
  exchange's fee) and against the next best price it was given; the average is shown.
- **Client record.** A banker sees how many deals a client has done on the Floor and how often the share moved its way
  in the half hour after: a client who is usually right is priced more carefully.
- **Axes.** A banker advertises what the desk wants to buy or sell, for the rest of the day; clients see it beside the
  desk's name.
- **For the empire.** The head and the Asset Managers may ask for prices for their empire. Every limit an empire's
  order meets applies: the member's trading limit, the 30% rule, no dealing with its own people, fair dealing for an
  empire the computer runs, and the Risk Manager's gate (a deal that would have to wait for the Risk Manager cannot be
  done on the Floor, where prices hold for seconds). A banker cannot deal with an empire they sit in.

Where: migration `056_floor_deeper.sql`; `simulation/floor.py`; the routes `POST /api/floor/ask-basket|ask-bond|counter|
answer|axe|chat|ticket|ticket/answer`, `GET /api/floor/bonds`. Tests: `tests/test_floor.py` (6 more), and the ticket
reading in `web/src/floor.test.ts`.

## Slice 4: the Floor in live events, the floor hour, and the Floor's wrongs

- **Live events on the Floor.** The game speaks on the Floor's news feed: at the bell ("Banks: make sure you give fast
  and competitive prices to your clients. Asset managers: make sure you get your cash working."), halfway, five minutes
  before the end, and each time the featured share has moved another 2% from the bell. The bar along the top gains a
  **Live event** figure: what the event's shares have done to what the player holds, since the bell.
- **Scoring.** Deals on the Floor in the touched shares count in an empire's result, like trades on the exchange (with no
  exchange fee). When the event is scored, the three banks that earned most from the gap in their prices (against the
  market's middle when asked) and the three clients who saved most against the exchange (with its fee) earn their
  empires 5, 3 and 1 league points. The past event shows them by name and place, never what they made.
- **The floor hour.** Every trading day from 18:00 to 19:00 Lagos time: the computer clients call the open desks twice
  as often (as they do during a live event), the Floor says so, and at the end it names the hour's best three banks and
  clients.
- **Fishing for prices.** A banker sees how many of a client's last ten requests were dealt. A client who asked eight
  or more times and dealt none is fishing: the desk is told, and the computer banks quote them twice as wide.
- **The Floor's wrongs, at the tribunal** (three new kinds of case; players may do them to one another, and pay for
  them):
  - **trading ahead of a client**: while a client's request was open to their desk, the banker traded the share on the
    exchange on the client's side, then dealt with the client at a worse price. The profit is the claim, disgorged, with
    a fine of the same again;
  - **passing on a client's order**: while the request was open, the banker wrote the share's code in the Floor chat to
    somebody else, who then traded on the client's side. Taken to be worth 2% of what that somebody traded;
  - **banks agreeing their prices**: two banks that both priced the same request wrote to each other while it was open.
    The regulator alone brings it; the judgment also bans trading for two quarters.

  The regulator finds them in the books as it finds the others; the wronged client may bring the first two. The
  tribunal's pages name the new kinds.
- **Proven Talent** gains an **On the Floor** section: deals as a client and the average against the exchange, deals won
  as a bank, deals it could not deliver, prices it backed away from, and places won in live events and floor hours.

Where: migration `057_floor_live.sql`; `simulation/floor.py` (`notes_tick`, `live_scores`, `hour_tick`, `fishing`,

Tests: `tests/test_floor.py` (4 more).

## Honest limits

- The Floor trades shares, baskets and bonds; options, futures and currencies are not on it yet.
- The banks' wrongs are found from patterns in the books (a trade on the client's side while the request was open, the
  share's code in the chat, two banks chatting during the same request). A banker who hedges honestly in that window can
  be named; the tribunal then decides on the number, as it does for every case.
- A computer bank has no limit of its own: it deals from the institutions' books, never offering what they do not hold.
