# The economy upgrade (12 October 2026)

The owner accepted this stage on 12 October ("accept all"), and said "ok now lets build whats next" after seeing the checklist.

It comes after the redesign and before Stage 8, so that Stage 8's rents and property demand can be built on the new consumers.

**A new world is needed for this version.** The households changed shape, and the rest of the world needs its opening money. An old world is refused plainly when its next quarter tries to close: "Start a new world to run this version".

## What changed

### Part A: 1,000 consumer groups
- **Who they are.** The five big household blocks are now 1,000 groups, shared by population: Lagos 350, Kano 200, and Abuja, Port Harcourt and Aba 150 each. Each group has:
  - one location;
  - one of five income levels (low to high);
  - one of the ten mindsets;
  - its own savings and debts;
  - its own taste for each kind of good.
- **How they spend.** Each quarter each group decides for itself:
  - how much of what it earned to spend (poorer groups spend nearly all of it; the richest save much more);
  - how much of its savings to dip into;
  - how keen it is to borrow (its mindset decides how it reacts to the news);
  - what to buy (poorer groups lean to food and necessities; richer ones to travel, hotels, property and finance).
- **The money stays exact.** Every location's money is still in one ledger account, and its debts are still in the banks' records. A group's savings and debts are its share of those. They are put back in line with the ledger, to the kobo, every time the groups decide.
- **Moving.** A few groups move each quarter, about five on average. They move towards places where jobs grow faster, and as many move each way otherwise.
  - Their savings are paid across through the ledger, and their debts go with them.
  - Each location's share of the population, and its mix of mindsets, follow the groups.
- **What players see.** The Economy page shows a new panel, "Households, by income and by mindset":
  - income, spending, share of spending, savings and debts by income level;
  - spending by mindset;
  - how many groups moved, and from where to where.
  - **No single group is ever shown.**

### Part B: the rest of the world (a simple version; the crises stage builds on it)
- **Oil price** (starts at $75 a barrel, a game figure).
  - It is set at each quarter's close for the next quarter.
  - It drifts back towards its usual level, swings with the world's secret randomness, and jumps when an event hits energy supply.
  - It is now what energy costs at home: the oil price in naira.
- **The naira's exchange rate** (starts at ₦1,500 to the dollar, a game figure).
  - It is set at the same moment.
  - It follows prices at home against prices abroad over time.
  - It moves with oil, with trade, with interest rates, with how many naira the rest of the world holds, and with a random swing. Never more than 15% in one quarter.
  - Each quarter's move comes with its main cause, in words. The cause always pushed the same way the naira actually moved.
- **Trade.**
  - **Imports.** Companies buy part of the goods they need abroad: manufactured goods, fuel, farm produce, cement and consumer goods. Never power, telecoms, transport or banking. When the naira is weak they buy less abroad and more at home.
  - **Exports.** The rest of the world buys oil and gas (three quarters of exports on day one), and some farm produce and factory goods. It buys more of those when the naira is cheap.
  - **Royalty.** The Government takes a fifth of what oil sold abroad brings in. It is part of what the Government collects and spends.
  - **Filings.** Oil and gas companies' filings say how much they sold abroad.
- **A weaker naira raises prices.** Goods bought abroad become dearer, which feeds into inflation.
- **Foreign investors.** A fourth computer-run investor, with money sent in from the rest of the world.
  - **When they come and go.** They bring money in when Nigerian interest rates pay well over lending abroad, after the naira's expected fall. They take it out when rates don't.
  - **How they trade.** They trade only through ordinary orders on the exchange. When keen, they price shares at most 1.5% above worth (or below, when leaving).
  - **The daily limit.** They stop pushing, and take their orders off for the day, once they have traded about 5% of what everybody else trades on an average recent day.
  - **Other events.** They accept good takeover offers as the pension funds do, and they vote with the board.
- **The rest of the world's money.** It is one ledger account. It started with an opening balance (four quarters of imports), like everybody else's opening money. It is paid for imports and spends on exports and on the foreign investors' money.
- **What players see.** The Economy page shows a new panel, "The rest of the world":
  - this quarter's oil price and exchange rate, with the change;
  - the cause of the naira's move;
  - charts of oil and the naira;
  - what was sold and bought abroad;
  - the Government's oil share;
  - foreign money in and out;
  - foreign investors' shares.
  - Elsewhere: "Where the money sits" has a new line, "Abroad", and the company page's owners list has "Foreign investors".
- **News.** Six new kinds of story, 54 new wordings:
  - oil up or down by 8% or more;
  - the naira weaker or stronger by 3% or more;
  - foreign money in or out by a fifth of what they hold or more.
  - The old "energy" commodity story is not printed when oil has its own story, so nothing appears twice.

## The ten-question loophole check (`tests/test_economy_upgrade_loopholes.py`, 11 tests, all pass)
1. **Can a player see one group's figures?** No. Only totals are sent, each covering 50 or more groups, and no server route reads the groups' table except for the added-up summary.
2. **Can money appear or vanish through groups, moves, trade or foreign money?** No. Every one of those moves naira between two accounts. The rest of the world got its money once, at the start. The ledger check and the money count are clean.
3. **Can anybody learn next quarter's oil price or rate early?** No. Nothing about a later quarter is stored until its close, and the world's secret is never sent.
4. **Can foreign investors get shares or set a price other than by trading?** No. Their shares only ever move through exchange trades and the usual company events.
5. **Can a player milk the foreign investors?** No. Buying from them and selling straight back always loses at least their margin plus the exchange's fee both ways.
6. **Can the foreign investors swamp the market?** No. Once their day's share is traded, they stop and take their orders off. Measured over two hours of trading among the computer-run investors, where they had no players to trade with: about 4% of the value traded.
7. **Can anybody trade as, or pay into, the rest of the world's account?** No. It cannot place orders, and the ledger refuses to overdraw it.
8. **Can the groups' savings and debts drift away from the ledger?** No. They match it to the kobo whenever the groups decide.
9. **Can an old world run half-working?** No. A world without groups, or without the rest of the world, is refused with a plain message.
10. **Can oil or naira news be read before its figures are public?** No. It is dated in the quarter the figures belong to, from its third day. Every printed oil price matches the close that set it.

## The independent review (10 findings, all acted on)
| # | Finding | What was done |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | An energy supply event moved oil three times as much, so inflation rose more than before | Now one-for-one; inflation is back in its old range |
| 2 | Moving groups would slowly even out all five locations | Destinations now include staying put, so moves balance both ways; tested |
| 3 | New loans were counted twice in a group's budget | Fixed: a group dips only into savings it had before the loan |
| 4 | The spending mix of mindsets was off the design (rich groups weigh more) | Mindsets are now dealt so the mix holds for spending; tested within 1.5 points |
| 5 | The naira's stated cause could contradict its move | The cause is now chosen among causes pushing the same way; tested over every quarter |
| 6 | The foreign investors' keenness was almost always at its top | Smaller, and it fades as they buy; top keenness halved to 1.5% |
| 7 | Players trading with themselves could raise the foreign investors' daily limit; the limit was re-read for every share | Self-trades no longer count; the limit is read once per round and lowered as they trade |
| 8 | Foreign investors were called "small investors" in control figures and never accepted takeovers | Named properly; they accept good offers as the pension funds do |
| 9 | Debts moved with a group used figures read before earlier moves that quarter | Re-read for every move |
| 10 | Some tests checked little | New tests added: balanced moves, cause matches the move, energy timing, the real daily limit, a round trip losing money, a world without the rest of the world |

## Honest limits
- **The ten-year run doesn't include trading days.** So the foreign investors held no shares in it. Their trading was measured separately (about 4% of trading). Their real effect on prices will only show with players in a live world.
- **The direction foreign investors lean can be guessed** from public interest rates, inflation and the news. A player who follows it can sell to them a little above worth (or buy a little below). The edge is at most 1.5%, and they trade only about 5% of the day. This is "follow the foreign money", and it is meant to reward reading the economy.
- **Foreign investors don't take up rights issues,** so they are diluted when a company offers new shares.
- **Energy's naira price uses next quarter's oil with this quarter's exchange rate.** The rate for the next quarter is only set after inflation is measured, so energy lags the naira's move by one quarter.
- **Moves still change population shares a little.** Kano 20% became 18.3%, and Port Harcourt 15% became 16.6%, in ten years.
- **The rest of the world is one simple buyer and seller.** Other countries, other currencies and world crises come with the crises stage.

- **Spending by mindset is now public, as totals.** The checklist the owner approved showed spending by mindset. Before this stage the game hid mindsets entirely. Players can now see the country's mix of mindsets and how much each spends, but still not how each one reacts, nor any location's own mix.

- Older tests changed, each on purpose:
  - **Two tests that relied on one particular company being picked.** The world now develops a little differently, so they now pick a company that suits the test:
    - the five closing prices;
    - a deal large enough to need a fairness opinion, and an offer "far above" both price and worth.
  - **A test listing every kind of payment.** It now includes the new ones: imports, exports, the oil royalty, foreign money and moves.
  - **A test listing who may own a private company.** It now includes the pension funds, which can win a sale.

  - **The test that hid mindsets entirely.** It now allows the country's spending by mindset, which this stage shows by the owner's approval. How each mindset reacts, and each location's own mix, stay hidden.
