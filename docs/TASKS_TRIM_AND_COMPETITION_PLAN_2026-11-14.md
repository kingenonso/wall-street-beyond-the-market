## Making the work competitive

The game's aim is to compete with other empires and players. Most tasks today are marked alone: you do the work, the
game marks it, you are paid. The proposal keeps a small core like that for learning, and makes most of the rest
something with a winner.

1. **Contested mandates (Investment Banker).**
   - A company that wants to raise money, sell a division or find a buyer invites every empire to pitch.
   - Each empire's banker names a price and terms. The company takes the best for it, as real companies do.
   - The winner gets the fee and league points; the others get nothing.
   - Mandates come from what is really happening: a company short of cash, an owner selling, a foreign parent leaving.
2. **Ranked work (all three builds).**
   - Everyone given the same company in the same quarter is ranked against each other: whose valuation was closest, whose
     risk call was right, whose forecast missed least.
   - The closest gets the most points.
   - A public table each quarter names the sharpest analysts.
3. **Work aimed at rivals.**
   - An Asset Manager who finds a rival empire's company is overvalued can sell it short, and the court of public opinion
     (and the regulator) decides who was right.
   - An Investment Banker defends a client against a rival's takeover.
   - A Risk Manager spots a rival bank's weak point before the market does.
   - Your work costs another empire.
4. **The empire league is the scoreboard.**
   - Everything won adds to the empire's standing. Mandates won, ranked results, takeovers won and lost, cases won in
     court and money made all count.
   - Personal levels stay, but the league table is where empires see who is ahead.
5. **A protected start.**
   - Up to Level 5, a player's work is marked alone, as now, so a beginner learns before meeting experienced empires.

### Rules that keep it fair (each will get a loophole test)
- Nobody pitches against their own empire, or ranks against a second account of their own.
- Two empires cannot agree to take turns winning: a hidden pact to share mandates is a case for the courts, as pacts
  already are.
- A ranked answer is sealed until the quarter closes, so nobody copies a better analyst's figure.
- A client chooses by its own interest, worked out from the record, never by who asked first.
