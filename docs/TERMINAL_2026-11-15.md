# The terminal, 15 November 2026

The desk is now a terminal: one company in the middle, the whole economy around it. Branch `terminal`.

## What is on the screen

**Left column, on every page: Market indicators.** Each of the eight indicators now shows:
- its figure and its move, as a chip;
- a small line of its last sixteen quarters;
- a scale with a mark for where it stands today and a shaded band for its usual range (the middle half of its own
  record; for inflation, what the Central Bank accepts);
- one plain sentence on what the move means for a player.

The column is 300 pixels wide (it was 260) to carry this.

**The desk (the middle).**
- The company: price, today's move, a candle chart of up to 48 trading days with the shares traded underneath, and the
  order book (who is waiting to sell, the last trade, who is waiting to buy).
- The monitor: your watchlist, or today's biggest moves when you keep none. Choosing a row moves the whole wall.
- Every industry today: a tile for each industry, wider for a bigger one, coloured by its move.
- Money on the move: the five cities in the last closed quarter, with company tax flowing to the Government in Abuja and
  the Government's money flowing back to households, and a table of every figure.
- Your money today: what was paid to you and by you (kept from the old desk).

**Beside it.**
- Why it moved: the exchange's own account of the chosen share's day, and the reasons on the record.
- The duel: your empire against the one next to it in the league (without an empire, the two at the top), on league
  points, competition points, worth, trust and reputation.
- The wire: the latest stories; a company's code on a story moves the wall to that company.
- Your watchlist, as before.

## What the server sends that it did not before

- `/api/readout`: each indicator's record, scale, position, usual range and meaning.
- `/api/economy/flows`: the last closed quarter, city by city (households' spending and wages, company tax by head
  office, the Government's payments, jobs). It is the published record: nothing of any player's is in it.

No database change.

## 16 November 2026: commands, trading from the terminal, and your money against the BSX 50

**The command line.** Across the top of the wall. Type, press Enter; `/` anywhere on the desk puts the cursor there.
While you type, the lines underneath say what each matching command would do (arrow keys to move, Tab to complete,
Enter or a click to run).

| Type | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| `HMB GO` or `HMB` | Harmattan Bank goes on the wall: chart, order book, why it moved, the trade form |
| `BUY HMB 500`, `SELL DSW 1,200 AT 9.50` | the trade form is filled in for you. **Nothing is sent** until you press its button |
| `RIPPLE HMB` (or `WHY HMB`) | why the share moved, brought into view |
| `HELP P/E`, `HELP market value` | the game's own plain-words meaning, with its example |
| `HELP` | every command |
| `GOV GO`, `MARKET`, `NEWS`, `ECO`, `PF`, `EMPIRE`, `RIVALS`, `RANK`, `WORK`, `INTEL`, `BORROW`, `FX`, `PROPERTY`, `COURT` | opens that page |

A mistake is answered in words ("ZZZ is not a share on the exchange", "How many HMB shares?", "Did you mean P/E?").
The reading of a typed line is its own file (`web/src/command.ts`) with its own tests (`command.test.ts`).

**Trading from the terminal.** The rail now starts with the same trade panel as a company's page, for the share on the
wall: buy or sell, at your price or the best price, for yourself or (if your seat may) your empire, with your waiting
orders and cancel. "Buy or sell" under the chart jumps to it.

**Your money against the BSX 50.** The net worth chart has a second, dashed line: your first figure on the chart,
grown or shrunk exactly as the BSX 50 was since. Under it: "You +6.5% · BSX 50 +1.8% · ahead of the market". The
server works out both lines and both moves (`index_kobo` on each point, `net_worth_change_bps`, `index_change_bps`).
A record that moved only a little is drawn from just under its lowest point, so the two lines can be told apart.
Your figure counts everything that came in and went out (pay and fees too), and the chart says so when you hover.

undid the phone rule). It now lies flat at the foot of the page, as intended.
