# Unions and strikes

The owner said "build" on 16 November 2026 and chose computer-run union leaders.

## Plan, in four slices

1. **Unions with real causes** (this slice). There is one union for each industry. Staff anger comes from the quarter's
   real events. Demands have deadlines, and computer-run boards answer them. Strikes cut sales. Pay deals raise the wage bill.
2. **The empire at the table.** In a company an empire controls, the head answers the demand: agree, offer less, or
   refuse. The head can ask for more time or bring in a mediator. Strikes and morale at private companies join the same system.
3. **Union crimes.** A rival can bribe a union leader to strike your company, or you can bribe one to call a strike off.
   The chance of being told on starts low and rises with each repeat. There will be a new court case kind, and
   journalists can expose it.
4. **Strike waves and the general strike.** Industry-wide strikes will come from causes, not chance. A rare general
   strike closes the exchange for at most two real days, with orders held in a queue. The Government Leader answers it.
   There will be prediction questions on strikes.

## Slice 1: how it works

**Unions.** Every industry except banking and insurance has a union, with its name, its leader and the leader's temper
(`world_data.UNIONS`). For example, the Mine Workers' Union is quick to strike and the Tech Workers' Union is patient.

**Why staff get angry.** At each quarter's close, the staff of every listed company weigh up the quarter:

| Cause | Anger |
| --- | --- |
| Prices rose faster than pay over the year (the same for everybody) | 3 points for each point of the gap |
| Profit up 20% or more on a year ago, while pay stood still | 1,500 |
| Jobs cut by more than 3% in the quarter | 3 points for each point cut |
| A dividend paid while pay fell behind | 500 |
| A pay deal already in force | minus 2 points for each point above the going rate |
| A strike in the last two quarters (nobody is paid on strike) | minus 3,000 |

The leader's temper scales the total. At 3,000 or more, the union lodges a demand for the coming quarter: a rise of
3% to 15%. The demand is made public on a day in the coming quarter, and nobody can see it before that day. The board
has seven game days to answer, which is about thirteen real hours.

**The answer.** A computer-run board weighs a year of the higher wage bill against what a strike would cost it. A
strike costs the sales it loses, at a 30% margin. The board offers the rise at which the two costs are equal, and never
more than the demand. How close an offer must be depends on the leader: a calm leader takes half the demand, and the
most militant takes only the full rise.

**The strike.** It lasts 4 to 21 game days and is recorded as the quarter's event `company_strike`. The days lost come
off the company's sales when the quarter closes. The strike ends in a deal halfway between the demand and the offer.
Every deadline and strike is settled before the quarter's events are read, so none is missed.

**The deal.** The company's direct labour and running costs rise by the deal (`companies.union_pay_bps`, at most 40%).
The going rate closes a quarter of the gap each quarter.

**No random strikes for listed companies.** Chance no longer draws a strike for a company whose union decides. The
industry-wide `sector_strike` is still drawn at random until slice 4.

**The news.** There are four new kinds: a demand, a deal, a strike, and a strike's end. Each has three wordings in each paper's voice.

**The screen.** The new tab is Economy → Unions. It shows labour now (prices against pay, demands open, strikes now,
deals in force), the disputes now with their reasons, how it works, the unions, the deals in force and earlier disputes.
The command `UNION` opens it.

**Money.** Nothing here moves money directly. A strike changes sales and a deal changes costs, and the company's own
quarter plays out through the ledger.

## Tests

`tests/test_unions.py` checks:
- every industry outside finance has a union, and chance never draws a strike for those companies;
- anger has causes, and a demand stays out of sight until its day;
- the board agrees, or the union strikes, and the strike cuts sales and ends in a deal;
- a deal makes staff dearer and fades;
- the page needs a login.

The news count test now counts 160 kinds.
