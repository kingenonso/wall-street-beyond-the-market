# The gap stage: finishing Stages 7, 8 and 9 before Stage 10

The owner approved this stage on 15 October 2026 with "lets build all the gap stage with your suggestions before
moving to stage 10". The plan is `notes/gap-stage-plan.md`; every suggestion in it was used. Voice chat is not part of
it (it needs the owner's choice of a paid service).

It was built in three parts. Each part had ten loophole tests and an independent review, and every review finding
was fixed with its own test. Nothing on the plan was dropped or put off.

Tests at the end: TESTS_LINE

## Things worth knowing, said plainly

1. **Players' option and future orders now meet each other.** An order is matched first against other players'
   waiting orders, then against the market makers' quotes. A player's own waiting order is never matched against
   them. An order that waits holds its cash; if the owner can no longer pay when it would match, it is cancelled.
2. **When a loser cannot pay**, their margin goes first, then the default fund (10% of every derivatives fee). What
   the fund paid is owed back to the fund; anything still missing is owed to the winners, shared by what each won.
3. **An earn-out is paid in proportion.** If the company reaches its target, the whole earn-out is paid; below it,
   the share it reached. If the company stops reporting, the earn-out is paid in full (the buyer cannot avoid it by
   hiding the results). A buyout using mezzanine or an earn-out still needs at least 10% of the price from the
   empire's own cash.
4. **Swaps have a ceiling.** What a side can lose on its swaps is at most five times what it is worth, and nobody in
   default may sign one. An empire cannot sign a swap with one of its own members.
5. **Farmland and resource land are rezoned by the planners alone.** An owner may ask to rezone other land (a
   ₦500,000 fee). When a rezoning is granted, half the gain in value is paid to the Government as a betterment
   levy. The planners also rezone land themselves when a district has been full for two quarters.
6. **A claim found by a search binds whoever owns the property at the next close**, so it cannot be dodged by a
   quick sale. A claim nobody searched for stays hidden on a property company's land, and comes to light on
   anyone else's.
7. **Home prices were calmed after the ten-year run.** In the first run, buying pushed home prices about 45% above
   other property and kept them there. The push from buying was cut from a fifth to 14% of what was lent. In the
   second run (below), homes stay close to other property and still fall when the rate rises.
8. **Work past a desk's budget asks the head.** The member is told at once; the head (or the deputy, after the usual
   wait) sees the desk's budget, what is used and any other waiting requests. A refusal stands for the quarter. An
   approval lapses at the quarter's close, or if the member has left the desk. The staff never approve these on
   their own.

## The ten-year run

Forty quarters were run with all of it in play: computer-run players traded options and futures with each other,
signed swaps with banks and with each other, bought preferred shares and warrants, bid at auctions, bought and sold
fund units, rezoned, developed and sold property privately through the twelve steps. Every quarter the books
balanced to the kobo (the audit and every new check found nothing), and no company had money it could not explain.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 5 | Year 7 | Year 10 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Central Bank rate | 12.0% | 14.0% | 13.0% | 13.0% | 13.0% | 13.5% |
| Inflation | 5.3% | 7.2% | 6.0% | 4.6% | 6.9% | 7.6% |
| Home prices (start = 10,000) | 9,613 | 8,855 | 12,869 | 14,734 | 18,066 | 24,684 |
| All property prices | 9,968 | 10,432 | 11,695 | 13,332 | 16,366 | 21,087 |
| Home loans gone bad that quarter | ₦3.1bn | ₦7.3bn | ₦2.8bn | ₦2.6bn | ₦5.4bn | ₦5.9bn |

The design's test held: each time the rate rose (Year 2, Year 7, Year 10), more home loans went bad, repossessions
rose and home prices fell back. Over ten years home prices ended about 17% above other property (45% in the first
run, before the change described above).

Also over the ten years: ten public works (about one a year); 181 rezonings; 1,400 home-loan losses taken by the banks;
22 preferred-share issues and 19 warrant issues; 172 swaps; the default fund collected ₦20.8m in fees and covered one
loss of ₦3.3m, which was paid back; the property trust grew from ₦1.6bn to ₦2.4bn. Eighteen players ended owing a
shortfall they could not pay (₦82m between them), which stays as their debt.
