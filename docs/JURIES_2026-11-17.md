# The tribunal's juries

The owner said "build tribunal first" on 17 November 2026. Everything is on the existing Tribunal page (News → Tribunal),
with no new tabs.

## Part 1: juries of players

**The defendant's three answers.** Before, a defendant could settle or fight, and fighting meant the judge found the
whole claim proved. Now the choices are:
- **Settle:** 60% of the claim, paid at once (unchanged).
- **Let the judge decide:** the whole claim proved, at the standard penalty (as before).
- **Ask for a jury:** with an optional statement to the jury of up to 600 characters.

**Who sits.** Five players are drawn, by a draw nobody can know in advance. A juror:
- has a career at Level 3 or above;
- is not either side;
- does not share an empire with either side;
- is not banned from trading;
- is not defending a case of their own.

If fewer than three may sit, the judge decides at once.

**The case file.** Jurors read the claim, the lines of the record that prove the number, the defendant's statement,
and the claimant's statement. A player who brought the case may put a statement before the jury while it sits.

**The vote.** Each juror votes once, and a vote cannot be changed. They decide two things:
- how much of the claim the record proves: all of it, three quarters, half, a quarter, or none;
- the penalty: lenient (half the fine, half the ban), standard, or harsh (the fine and a half, and two more quarters of ban).

A juror may give a short reason. It is shown with their vote, but without their name.

**The verdict.** The middle vote decides each question. With an even count, the lower middle decides, so doubt goes to
the defendant. The jury closes when all five have voted, or after 24 real hours.
- **Part proved:** disgorgement, the fine and costs are worked out on the proved part. The claim itself is never
  rewritten: the court's guard still holds.
- **None proved (acquitted):** the defendant owes nothing.
  - A player who brought the case loses the bond to the defendant, as the costs of defending.
  - If the regulator brought it, it pays the defendant's costs from its budget, as far as it can.
- **Fewer than three votes by the deadline:** the judge decides.

**Jurors are paid.** The Treasury pays ₦250,000 to each juror who voted, even if the judge ends up deciding. A vote that
matches the verdict (the same finding, and the same penalty unless acquitted) earns a point of reputation.

**In the open.**
- The docket shows a sitting jury and how many have voted.
- "Recent jury verdicts" shows every vote and its reason, without names.
- A juror's own "Jury duty" panel shows their cases, votes cast, votes that matched the verdict, and fees earned.
- A "Needs you now" card tells a juror they have a vote to cast.

**Website.**
- `POST /api/court/cases/{id}/answer` now takes `{choice: settle|fight|jury, defence}`.
- `POST /api/court/cases/{id}/vote` takes `{proved_bps, penalty, reason}`.
- `POST /api/court/cases/{id}/note` takes `{note}`.
- Migration `066_juries.sql`.

**Tests** (`tests/test_juries.py`):
- a jury of five with no tie to either side;
- the middle vote finds half proved, and the jurors are paid;
- an acquittal sends the claimant's bond to the defendant;
- too few votes, or too few who may sit, and the judge decides;
- votes must be one of the choices, and outsiders cannot vote;
- the routes need a login and good input.

## Ideas for part 2 (not built; for the owner to choose)
- **Jury tampering:** offering a juror money for a vote, as a crime between players with the same "chance of being told"
  rule as union bribes.
- **Challenges:** each side may strike out one juror and have another drawn.
- **Appeals:** a losing side pays to have a fresh jury hear the case.
